A robotic arm that helps people feed themselves.

Munchkin picks up a bite from your plate and brings it to your mouth when you're ready, so people who can't easily lift a spoon can eat on their own, at the table with everyone else.

We're building it in Needham, Massachusetts. It works, but it isn't for sale yet.

Why we're building it

Eating happens three times a day, every day. For people living with ALS, a spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy and many other conditions, each of those meals usually depends on someone else holding the spoon.

Feeding devices do exist, but most of the ones on the market still need a person next to them to load the spoon, line it up, or press a button for every bite. The helper ends up feeding the machine instead of eating with the person. They are also expensive.

We want a device that does the whole job by itself, so the person using it controls the pace and the family can sit down and eat at the same time. And we want it to cost a lot less.

How it works

  1. It scoops a bite

    A camera on the arm finds the food on the plate or in the bowl.

  2. It brings it to you

    The same camera follows your face, so the spoon goes to your mouth even if you move.

  3. It waits for you

    It holds still until you've taken the bite, then goes back for the next one.
